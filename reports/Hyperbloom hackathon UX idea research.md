# Design India's Scam Pause to Win Hyperbloom

Use the ChatGPT report only as a basic checklist. Do not build on it. Its WCAG numbers and its Spotify fact are correct. But several company facts are old or have no source, its citations are dead internal markers, and its five ideas run into big government apps launched in 2025–26. Hyperbloom October is a single-track "UI/UX and Web Design" hackathon. It is judged on six areas: UX and user flows, visual UI, accessibility and inclusive design, creative interaction and information architecture, problem-solving and originality, and polish. No weights are published. The deadline is **19 Oct 2026, 5:00pm EDT, which is Tuesday 20 Oct, 02:30 IST**. The cash pool is **US$9,117** plus Deployxa credits, and every member must be a student above the age of majority (18+ in India). Our one recommendation is to build **"Ruko"** (a working name). It is a mobile-first safety layer that helps older Indians pause, check and get family help during a "digital arrest" scam call, and act fast in the "golden hour" if the money is already gone. It has the strongest evidence of harm (**₹1,935 crore lost to digital arrest in 2024**), a vivid demo, a natural accessibility story, and an angle few teams take, because most student scam apps are "paste a message, get a verdict" checkers. The runner-ups are an accessible e-KYC flow for blind and low-vision users, and a scholarship pre-flight checker. Drop the chatbot, transit and learning-hub ideas. Ship one polished hero flow as a Figma prototype and a live frontend on Deployxa, plus a video of 3 minutes or less and a case study that follows the six criteria. One warning applies to everything below: the network blocked Devpost during research, so every rule here came from search-engine extracts. The team must check them on the live Rules page.

## The GPT report is a good checklist but a weak foundation

The parts that hold up are the generic UX basics. The WCAG 2.2 numbers are right: **4.5:1** contrast for normal text, 3:1 for large text, and a new **24×24 CSS px** minimum target size ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum); [W3C target size](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/blob/main/guidelines/sc/22/target-size-minimum.html)). The Spotify fact is also right: Spotify had **22 separate design systems** before Encore launched in November 2019. Encore is a "system of systems", not one big library ([Figma blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/creating-coherence-how-spotifys-design-system-goes-beyond-platforms/)). The PDF's list of pitfalls (vague problems, no personas, half-finished flows, ignored accessibility, wordy write-ups) matches what Devpost judges say. Many entries miss basic requirements, and "ambiguity is a red flag" ([Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)). So sections B and C of the PDF work as a starter checklist.

## Industry is turning good UX into law, labels and numbers

The biggest 2024–26 shift is that accessibility stopped being optional. The **European Accessibility Act has been enforceable since 28 June 2025** for e-commerce, banking, transport ticketing and apps ([Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2025/07/european-accessibility-act-digital-products)). Apple now shows **Accessibility Nutrition Labels** on App Store listings, covering nine features such as VoiceOver, Larger Text, Sufficient Contrast and Reduced Motion ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/overview-of-accessibility-nutrition-labels/)). WCAG 2.2 added nine new success criteria, including 24 px targets, "focus not obscured" by sticky bars, a single-tap alternative to every drag, and no memory or puzzle tests at login ([W3C](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/tree/main/guidelines/sc/22)). US accessibility lawsuits passed **5,000 in 2025** ([Fox Rothschild](https://www.foxrothschild.com/events/ada-website-lawsuit-trends-what-2025-filings-mean-for-2026)). India moved too. On 30 April 2025 the Supreme Court held that digital access is part of the right to life and gave 20 directions to make e-KYC usable by persons with disabilities ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/supreme-court/right-to-digital-access-part-of-article-21-supreme-court-directs-to-make-ekyc-process-accessible-to-persons-with-disabilities-290778)). NCPEDP found **over 90% of Indian finance websites** fail accessibility standards ([People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/amp-diversity-over-90-indian-finance-websites-fail-accessibility-standards-43526)).

Big design systems went two ways at once. Google's **Material 3 Expressive** (May 2025) claims backing from 46 studies and 18,000+ participants. Google says people spotted key elements **up to 4× faster** and older users did as well as younger ones, though these figures are self-reported ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-material-3-expressive-details-3554486/)). Apple's **Liquid Glass** (June 2025) shows the cost of style over legibility. One agency measured some beta screens at about **1.5:1 contrast** ([Infinum](https://infinum.com/blog/apples-ios-26-liquid-glass-sleek-shiny-and-questionably-accessible/)), and Apple then added a Clear/Tinted choice in iOS 26.1 and a "Reduce Bright Effects" toggle in iOS 26.4 beta ([PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/liquid-glass-was-so-bad-that-apple-will-give-you-another-way-to-tone-it-down_id178795)). Others simplified. Shopify rebuilt Polaris as framework-agnostic web components, stable since 1 Oct 2025 ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/changelog/polaris-unified-web-components-are-now-stable)). Linear cut **98 theme variables down to 3 inputs**: base colour, accent and contrast ([Linear](https://linear.app/blog/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Fluent 2 splits tokens into raw global values and meaningful alias names, so light, dark and high-contrast themes come cheap ([Fluent 2](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/design-tokens)).

AI interfaces now have rulebooks. Apple's Generative AI guidelines say to keep people in control, clearly label AI, offer a non-AI fallback, and ask before any significant action. In 2026 Apple added two rules: put Edit, Undo and Retry next to generated content, and replace "Processing…" with specific copy like "Summarizing key themes from your notes" ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)). Microsoft's HAX toolkit lists 18 guidelines, such as "support efficient correction" ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/)). IBM's Carbon for AI puts a label on every AI instance and adds a "revert to AI" control ([IBM Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/carbon-for-ai/)). Research backs careful wording. In a 404-person experiment, first-person hedges like "I'm not sure, but…" cut over-reliance on wrong answers ([Kim et al., FAccT 2024](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2405.00623)). NN/g warns that people rarely click citations, so their mere presence inflates trust ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/explainable-ai/)). The EU AI Act's transparency duties started on 2 Aug 2026 ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-03-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-take-effect-2-august-2026)).

Regulators are also going after manipulative design. India's CCPA listed **13 banned dark patterns** in November 2023, including false urgency, basket sneaking, confirmshaming and subscription traps ([KS&K](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/safeguarding-consumer-rights-dark-patterns-guidelines-2023/)). In June 2025 it ordered e-commerce platforms to self-audit within three months ([AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/central-consumer-protection-authority-issues-advisory-to-e-commerce-platforms-for-self-audit-to-detect-dark-patterns-on-their-platforms/)), and penalties so far total about ₹20 lakh across 7 platforms ([The Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/ccpa-dark-pattern-cases-bring-20-lakh-in-penalties-and-interface-changes)). The DPDP Rules, notified in November 2025, require plain-language consent notices and make withdrawing consent as easy as giving it ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1708164/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified)). The research explains why this matters: removing "Reject" from the first screen of a cookie banner raised consent by about 22–23 points ([Nouwens et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.02479)).

Teams now measure UX with hard numbers. Google made **Interaction to Next Paint (INP)** a Core Web Vital on 12 March 2024 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/inp-cwv-march-12)). "Good" means INP of 200 ms or less, LCP of 2.5 s or less and CLS of 0.1 or less ([web-vitals](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/web-vitals)). Duolingo shows the value of small, tested tweaks: letting learners hold two Streak Freezes lifted daily active learners by 0.38% ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)).

India's own UX moves are about removing literacy, language and login barriers. NPCI's **Hello! UPI** brings voice payments, working with Bhashini toward 22 languages ([NPCI](https://npci.org.in/what-we-do/hello-upi/product-overview)). UPI Lite allows PIN-less payments up to ₹1,000 ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/upi-lite-rbi-raises-limit-of-wallet-to-rs-5000-per-transaction-to-rs-1000-3303602)). Since an NPCI circular of 7 Oct 2025, fingerprint or face approval can replace the UPI PIN for payments up to ₹5,000 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/personal-finance/news/story/no-more-pins-upi-to-get-face-and-fingerprint-authentication-for-faster-safer-online-payments-497815-2025-10-11)), and BHIM rolled this out in March 2026 ([IANS](https://ianslive.in/bhim-app-launches-biometric-authentication-for-upi-payments-up-to-rs-5000--20260324134230)). Swiggy and Sarvam AI launched voice ordering in 11 Indian languages, including a phone-call channel that needs no app or internet ([Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/swiggy-introduces-ai-voice-ordering-with-sarvam-ai-across-11-indian-languages/)). On the web, smooth screen-to-screen morphing became easy when same-document View Transitions reached all major browsers in October 2025 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available)). Calm design got a formal yardstick too: the Calm Tech Institute's 81-point certification asks that all but the most crucial notifications be off by default ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/calm-tech)).

## Build Ruko: a scam pause for elders and their families

The recommendation is **Ruko** ("stop" in Hindi; a working name, so search the app stores before you commit). Ruko is a mobile-first safety layer for people aged 60+ and their adult children. It covers three moments. **Before** a scam, the elder and family set up a trusted person and safety rules. **During** a "digital arrest" call, Ruko helps the elder pause, check and silently alert family. **After** money is lost, it walks them through the "golden hour" of reporting.

The harm is huge, recent and well documented. Indians lost **₹22,845.73 crore to cyber fraud in 2024**, up from ₹7,465.18 crore in 2023 ([The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/07/22/del23-lsq-cyber-fraud.html)). Digital-arrest scams alone took **₹1,935 crore across 1.23 lakh complaints** in 2024, and 17,718 more incidents came in just January–February 2025 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=504907)). The victims are often retired. An 85-year-old professor transferred ₹9 crore after a video call from a man in police uniform ([The Hitavada](https://www.thehitavada.com/Encyc/2025/12/25/85-yr-old-retired-engineering-professor-loses-rs-9-crore-to-digital-arrest-scam.html)). A 72-year-old in Surat lost ₹1.47 crore while scammers kept him isolated on continuous video calls ([Daily Pioneer](https://www.dailypioneer.com/news/72-year-old-surat-man-duped-of-rs-1-47-crore-in-digital-arrest-fraud)). HelpAge India names digital-arrest threats, AI bank calls and deepfakes of family members as the top scams against seniors in 2025 ([t2online](https://t2online.in/tech/interviews/interview-with-helpage-on-cybercrime-and-online-safety-for-senior-citizens-on-safer-internet-day/2003551)). The user base keeps growing: India will have **347 million people aged 60+ by 2050** ([Scroll/UNFPA](https://scroll.in/latest/1056724/indias-ageing-population-to-double-by-2050-says-un-report)). The problem is global too, which helps your pitch. Americans aged 60+ reported **$4.885 billion** in losses to the FBI in 2024 ([Erie News Now](https://www.erienewsnow.com/story/52849817/fbi-warns-of-rising-elder-fraud-ahead-of-awareness-day)).

Existing tools leave a gap that design can fill. Sanchar Saathi's Chakshu flags suspicious calls, but money fraud must be reported separately through 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in, which splits the journey ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/explainers/explainer-the-sanchar-saathi-specifics)). Speed decides outcomes. Mumbai police say most unrecovered money is lost because victims realise too late, while one firm got back ₹11.20 crore of ₹11.34 crore because staff called 1930 at once ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/04/13/bom9-mh-cyber-crime.amp.html)). Student builds on Devpost, such as ScamShield Seniors, are mostly "paste a message or URL, get a verdict" checkers ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/scamshield-seniors)). Those assume a calm, doubtful user. A digital-arrest victim is frightened, isolated and told to stay silent. Designing for that moment, and for a second person (the family), is fresh. There is also a timely policy hook. An RBI discussion paper proposes a **one-hour delay on transfers above ₹10,000** and **trusted-person approval for customers aged 70+ on transfers above ₹50,000** ([Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/indias-central-bank-mulls-delay-135740570.html)). Ruko can show what that rule could feel like if it were designed well.

Ruko also hits all six criteria naturally. Elders need large text, high contrast, simple language and voice, so accessibility is part of the product, not an add-on. The before/during/after structure is a clear information-architecture story. A role-played scam call is the most gripping 60 seconds of demo you can film. Everything runs on mock data, so the team's time goes into design, not bank integrations.

The **learning hub** depends on content you cannot make in nine days, in a crowded edtech space. The **college portal** and **rural banking** ideas are too broad as written; BHIM 3.0 already ships in 15+ languages ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/bhim-3-0-track-manage-split-expenses-with-support-in-15-languages-125032600331_1.html)). Also skip the usual crowded categories: food-waste trackers (one Devpost search found at least 9 ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/no-food-waste-ifvjcz))), mood journals, to-do apps, campus event apps, generic SOS buttons, generic AI chatbots and AQI dashboards.

Ruko has four risks, and each has a fix. First, "it's just another scam app": design for the moment of fear and the second person, and show the verdict checker as a rejected concept in your case study. Second, "a web app cannot see phone calls": simulate the incoming call in the prototype, and in "What's next" explain how a bank or UPI app could adopt the pattern. Third, scaring users: keep the tone warm and calm, and let the elder control what family sees. Fourth, research ethics: get consent, role-play only with willing participants, and never run a surprise "test scam" on anyone.

## One hero flow, scored against all six criteria

Build one continuous story and polish it until nothing breaks. A wide prototype that is only half clickable scores worse than one perfect flow.

| Step | Screen | What happens | Key design decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Setup (done calmly, with family) | Pick language, add a trusted person, set "calm-moment rules", agree on a family code word, try a 2-minute drill | The elder chooses the rules while calm, for their frightened future self |
| 1 | Simulated "CBI officer" video call | The elder taps the big Ruko button and the call keeps running | Victims are kept on the call, so Ruko must work without hanging up |
| 2 | Panic card | Big text, read aloud: "Real police never arrest anyone on a video call. You do not have to pay." Two buttons: "Tell my person" and "Check this call" | One idea per screen; calm words; no jargon |

| 4 | Transfer pause | Literal label: "You are sending ₹2,00,000 to a new person." Shows the matched scam script, the 1-hour lock the elder chose, and "Waiting for Rahul" | Friction only where money can be lost; "Cancel transfer" is the main button |
| 5 | Family view | The son gets one alert: "Maa opened Ruko during a call. Possible digital-arrest scam." Actions: Call, Approve, Block | A second person in the loop; only critical alerts |
| 6 | Golden hour | "Money already sent? Call 1930 now." Then an evidence checklist (transaction ID, amount, time, number, screenshots), an auto-built complaint pack, and a status timeline (freeze, police, refund order) | A countdown with clear steps beats a wall of instructions |

Some touches would be unique to Ruko, and they are where a team shows real UX thinking. These are our design proposals, built on the evidence above.

| Signature touch | Why it improves Ruko | What it builds on |
|---|---|---|
| **Calm-moment rules**: the elder sets limits while calm, e.g. "new person + over ₹10,000 = wait 1 hour and ask Rahul" | Self-chosen friction protects without taking away control | RBI's proposed delay and trusted-person rule ([Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/indias-central-bank-mulls-delay-135740570.html)) |

| **Golden-hour complaint pack**: auto-collects the evidence and explains each status in plain words | Victims panic and do not know what to collect | Mumbai's 1930 recovery case ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/04/13/bom9-mh-cyber-crime.amp.html)) |

| **"Judge tour" button** on the live site: a 60-second guided overlay that labels each design decision | Busy judges see your rationale without digging | Devpost: if you can't explain it in a sentence or two, it's too complex ([Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/understanding-hackathon-submission-and-judging-criteria)) |

Avoid the mistakes that knowledgeable judges notice. Do not ship OpenDyslexic as "the dyslexia fix"; the best controlled study found no gain in reading speed or accuracy ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5629233/)), and one October rival already uses it. Offer spacing and line-length controls instead. Do not use glass or blur effects that fail 4.5:1 contrast. Do not show status by colour alone, and do not let layouts change by themselves without user control. Do not report a SUS score from five users as a real result; NN/g says SUS needs 20–30 users to generalise ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/measuring-perceived-usability/)). Report task success and the top issues you fixed instead. Keep AI out of the core path. If you add an optional "explain this SMS" helper, label it as AI, hedge in first person when unsure, put Edit and Retry next to the output, and keep the non-AI script check as a fallback ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)).

Protect scope. The **must-haves** are steps 1–5 coded and in Figma, the "Adapt to me" panel, step 6 in Figma, and the accessibility audit. The **nice-to-haves** are the coded golden-hour flow, the complaint-pack PDF, the rehearsal drill and real voice. **Cut first** if you fall behind: the second regional language and a full family dashboard.

## Conclusion

The winning shape here is "designed like a case study, shipped like a product". The organisers allow Figma-only entries. But engineer judges, an explicit polish criterion, a sponsor prize for deploying, and rivals who already ship coded, accessible apps all reward a live build on top of the design work. Accessibility is the cheapest lever: one-sixth of the score in theory, yet it is the area most teams fake. A measured audit, a working high-contrast mode and five seconds of a screen reader on camera will separate you from teams that only claim WCAG compliance.

The deeper lesson of Ruko is that good UX is not always about making things faster. In safety design, the craft is deciding where to slow people down and making that friction one the user chooses, explains and controls. That idea links the RBI's proposals, India's dark-pattern rules and Apple's "act in people's best interest" principle, and few student teams will argue it. The GPT report shows the flip side of AI-assisted research. Its biggest problem was not a few wrong numbers. It was missing context: the real rules, the real rivals, and the 2025–26 shifts. Use AI to draft, check every claim against a primary source, and disclose your AI use openly in the submission.
